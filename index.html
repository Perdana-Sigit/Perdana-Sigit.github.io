Profile

I am an environmental and energy economist specialising in quantitative policy analysis. My work examines how climate and energy policies affect economic activity and welfare in interconnected economies.

My research investigates economic consequences that are not always apparent from a policy’s intended objectives or initial design. I examine adjustments across countries, sectors, regions, households, and trading partners, identifying the mechanisms behind outcomes that may be uneven, unexpected, or insufficiently understood.

Research Focus

My research focuses on five interconnected areas:

  • Climate Policy: Carbon pricing, emissions trading, international climate cooperation, and policy effectiveness.
  • International Trade & CBAM: Trade competitiveness, carbon leakage, and the economic implications of border carbon adjustments.
  • Regional & Distributional Impacts: Regional inequalities, household welfare, employment effects, and just transition policies.
  • Energy Transition: Decarbonisation pathways, energy security, and the economic implications of transforming energy systems.
  • Economic & Integrated Assessment Modelling: CGE and IAM applications, scenario analysis, and multi-model assessments of climate and energy policies.

Research Vision

My future research aims to investigate how climate and energy policies transmit through trade, production, labour markets, and regional economies—and why similar interventions can produce different economic outcomes across settings. I am particularly interested in indirect effects and economic interactions that remain insufficiently understood.

To address these questions, I draw on quantitative economic modelling, empirical methods, and spatial analysis, selecting and combining approaches according to the policy question. My goal is to generate new economic insights into the mechanisms and trade-offs that shape policy outcomes and inform academic and policy debates.
